What One More Day is

Tagline

Description

One More Day began as a place to put words to the things we all feel and rarely say. What started as a single page about love, loss, and healing has grown into a daily gathering of more than 219,000 people. The book carries that same heart — written by the same hands, for the same reasons.

The one-sentence promise

Whatever you are carrying, you are not carrying it alone.

Why this brand works. The audience did not arrive for an author or a product. They arrived because someone said the thing they could not say themselves. Every piece of content should earn its place the same way — comfort first, book second.

02 — Audience

Who we are speaking to

People who have loved and lost — and who read at night, on their phone, when the house is quiet.

Audience: Adults, skewing 35–70 and predominantly women, who have experienced the death of a spouse, parent, child, or close friend. They follow grief and hope pages daily, share quote cards with their own friends, and read fiction that helps them feel understood rather than fixed. Many are years into their loss and still find that no one talks about it with them.

What they want

  • To feel seen — words that name a feeling they have not been able to name.
  • Permission — to still be grieving, at their own pace, without apology.
  • Hope that is honest, never forced or cheerful.
  • Something to share, so their own people understand them a little better.

What they do not want

  • Being sold to in a moment of vulnerability.
  • Advice, stages of grief, or timelines.
  • Slogans that skip past the hard part to the silver lining.

They engage most with

  • Short quote cards, plainly set
  • Reels with a quiet, spoken line
  • Anything naming a specific, small moment of loss

They disengage from

  • Repeated promotion of the same link
  • Stock-photo positivity
  • Anything that sounds like a brand talking

03 — Voice & Tone

How One More Day sounds

Voice summary: Tender, plainspoken, and unhurried. One More Day speaks the way a trusted friend speaks late at night — honest about grief, never heavy for its own sake, and always ending somewhere a person can stand.

Voice adjectives

TenderHonestUnhurried WarmPlainspokenHopeful Quietly confidentNever saccharine

Sentences that sound right

Whenever grief tries to steal the beauty of your memories, just remember: Love. Never. Dies.
Letting go of something precious may be as simple as saying: ‘Thank you for the journey.’

Do

  • Write short. One thought per post.
  • Name the specific moment — the empty chair, the unsent text.
  • Let a post simply comfort, with nothing to click.
  • Speak to one person, not an audience.
  • End on hope that has been earned.
  • Use plain words a grieving person can absorb at 11pm.

Don’t

  • Don’t give advice or name stages of grief.
  • Don’t tell anyone how long healing should take.
  • Don’t use marketing language: unlock, must-read, don’t miss.
  • Don’t stack hashtags or use trending-topic hooks.
  • Don’t sell in the same breath as a grief post.
  • Don’t use exclamation marks or emoji in the voice of the brand.

The Cardinal rule. Cardinal red appears exactly once in any given piece — the balloon in the sky, or the single button that asks someone to buy the book. Never as a background, never for emphasis, never twice. Its power is that it is rare.

Using colour

  • Text is always Dusk Ink on Dawn Paper — never grey on white.
  • Backgrounds may be a soft dawn gradient (sky blue at top, warm cream at the bottom).
  • No pure black (#000) and no pure white (#FFF) anywhere in the brand.
  • Never place text over a busy photograph; give words their own quiet ground.

06 — Typography

The type system

Heading font — Cormorant Garamond

One More Day
Love. Never. Dies.

A literary serif with real warmth. Used for the title, all headings, and every quotation. Quotations are always set in the italic. Weight 600. Never use it for long paragraphs or small print.

Body font — Source Sans 3

Clear, warm, and easy to read at any size — including on a phone at night, which is when most of this audience reads. Weight 400 for text, 600 for buttons and labels.

UseFontNotes

07 — The Marks

Heart-cloud and balloon

The heart-cloud — primary mark

A soft white cloud shaped like a heart, on a circle of sky. It is the profile picture the audience already recognises. Keep it soft-edged and rounded — never a hard geometric heart icon.
  • Minimum size 32px. Always keep clear space of at least half its width on every side.
  • Do not recolour it, add a drop shadow beyond the soft one built in, rotate it, or place it on a busy photo.
  • On dark backgrounds, keep the sky circle — do not knock the mark out to white.

The balloon — secondary motif

A small cardinal-red hot-air balloon, high in the sky. It appears once, small, and unexplained — a quiet signature rather than a logo.
Handle with care. The balloon carries a personal meaning for the author. It is never a decoration, never repeated across a layout, never animated for attention, and never used in a promotional or jokey context. When in doubt, leave it out — and ask Chris.

08 — Imagery

What the pictures look like

Skies, water, and light at the edges of the day. Space to breathe. Almost never a face.

Yes

  • Dawn and dusk skies; clouds with light behind them
  • Shorelines, still water, open horizons
  • Silhouettes — hands, birds, a lone figure, seen small
  • Single objects with meaning: a chair, a rose, a letter
  • Soft focus, generous empty space, muted natural colour

No

  • Stock-photo smiling models or business imagery
  • Cemeteries, coffins, hospital beds, anything clinical
  • Heavy filters, high saturation, hard contrast
  • Cluttered collages or more than one focal point
  • Text crowded to the edges of the frame

Quote cards — the workhorse format

  • One thought per card. If it needs two sentences, it needs two cards.
  • Set the quote in the serif italic, centred, with wide margins.
  • Background: a soft dawn gradient or a quiet photograph at low contrast.
  • No logo lockup on every card — the voice is the signature. The mark appears occasionally, small.
  • Square (1:1) for feed, vertical (4:5 or 9:16) for stories and reels.

09 — Social

How we show up daily

The page posts every day and always has. The book does not change that rhythm — it lives inside it.

Content mix — the 5:1 rule

For every one post that asks for something (join the list, get the book), publish at least five that ask for nothing. This ratio is the reason the audience trusts the page, and it is not negotiable.

PillarShareWhat it is
Comfort~40%Quote cards naming a feeling. The core of the page.
Permission~20%Grief has no timeline; you are allowed to still be sad.
Hope~20%Light at the edge — earned, never forced.
The story~10%Glimpses of the book and the writing of it.
The ask~10%Join the list, and later: get your copy.

Rules of the room

  • Never post an ask on the same day as a post about a specific loss.
  • Replies matter more than posts. When someone shares their grief in a comment, answer them as a person — briefly, warmly, with no link.
  • Reels: one line, spoken plainly, over a quiet sky. No music that swells.
  • Never boost or run ads on a grief post. Ask-posts only.

Hashtags

Used sparingly — three to five, at the end, never mid-sentence.
